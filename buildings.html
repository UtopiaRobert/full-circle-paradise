Chapter 06 · Shelter, tools and shared infrastructure

Buildings & Energy

The farm’s built environment is informal, inventive and deeply tied to the community’s ability to maintain and adapt it.

A village rather than a single house

Full Circle Farm combines a main house with cabins, trailers, tents, work buildings and shared facilities. This distributed pattern allows privacy without giving up communal kitchens, bathrooms, tools and gathering spaces.

Natural building as proof

The small earth dome is more than an object of curiosity. Its thermal mass, local material palette and survival through wildfire show what low-impact construction can contribute in a hot, fire-prone landscape.

Energy begins with climate

Before adding equipment, the design prioritizes shade, ventilation, insulation and reduced demand. Wood heat makes use of a local maintenance product, while solar electricity can offset expensive grid power and strengthen emergency resilience.

Shared capacity

The workshop, tractor and tool collection are among the farm’s most important assets. A repaired hinge, sharpened blade or functioning pump may create more value than a new feature added elsewhere.

Design priorities

Improve what serves many people

Community room roof

02

03

04

Greywater-ready utilities

Connect laundry and bathing infrastructure to productive perennial landscapes.
