Climate & Ecology

A long dry summer, a short wet winter, wildfire, frost pockets and mountain winds shape every design decision.

Mediterranean rhythm

Most rain arrives in winter and early spring. Summer is reliably dry, so every productive system depends on stored soil moisture, irrigation, shade or drought-adapted vegetation.

The farm sits close enough to the mountains for local conditions to depart from broad Ojai averages. Cold air settles in low areas, while the raised northeastern end receives more breeze. Buildings and mature trees create a surprisingly varied patchwork of shade and heat.

Five useful microclimates

  1. Farm center: dense canopy and structures produce the coolest everyday living spaces.
  2. Vegetable garden: high solar exposure, with irrigation raising local humidity.
  3. Persimmon Hill: slightly elevated, breezier and comparatively dry.
  4. Hot and dry edges: exposed areas suited to tough, low-water species.
  5. Lion Creek banks: the wettest and least managed ecological edge.

Ecology as infrastructure

Mature shade trees, pollinators, creek vegetation and the soil food web perform work that machinery would otherwise have to provide. Some species also create problems: invasive trees, fuel accumulation and aggressive creek plants require persistent management.

Design response

Shade is a water strategy

Preserve the best existing canopy around homes and gathering spaces.

Replace hazardous or invasive trees gradually rather than removing all shade at once.

Use layered plantings, mulch and wind protection to reduce irrigation demand.
