Garden & orchard

The garden as Zone 1

The vegetable garden is visited every day, which makes it the correct place for crops requiring close attention. Its challenge is not location but organization: paths, tools, propagation space, irrigation and compost all need to support daily work rather than interrupt it.

Perennial abundance

Established citrus, persimmons, pomegranates, loquats and other fruit trees represent years of stored biological capital. Their care should focus on water efficiency, mulch, pruning, understory diversity and gradual succession where trees are declining.

Animals as workers

Chickens convert kitchen residues into eggs, manure and scratching behavior. Bees connect flowering plants across the property. Worms process selected food waste. Each system works best when its inputs are cleanly separated and easy to move.

Immediate improvement
Create one clear organic-waste station: chicken food, worm food, compost material and trash should never be confused.

Build soil, not dependency

Woody prunings can be chipped for paths and fungal mulch. Finished compost belongs where crops are most demanding. Perennial zones can rely more heavily on leaf litter, chop-and-drop biomass and nitrogen-fixing support species.

Drought-tolerant fruit

Pomegranate, persimmon, loquat, citrus, guava and carob diversify harvests.

Pollinator habitat

Flowers through the seasons support both managed bees and wild insects.

Woody biomass

The chipper turns a fire-management burden into mulch and soil cover.
